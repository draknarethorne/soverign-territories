# Retrospective: 2026-10-27 — intake feedback changed the acceptance bar

**Retrospective day:** 2026-10-27  
**Written:** 2026-10-29, 06:20 -05:00 (delayed; not a contemporaneous log)

## The day in a few lines

The work moved from a single accepted synthetic repair to repeatable intake
experiments across screenshot layouts. User review exposed that successful
hashes, masks and tests did not prove the actual recommendation was correctly
framed or selected. The flow was corrected, frozen samples were inspected, and
the overlay patterns became reusable data rather than one-image patches. No
full library rebase, matching install or production change was made. The
current state and remaining gates are in the
[intake plan](../plans/2026-10-23-image-intake-preparation.md) and
[cast-rebase plan](../plans/2026-10-23-cast-seeded-library-rebase.md).

## What was good

- The 46-source same-mask comparison kept 22 LaMa/classical-fill comparisons
  separate from 24 explicit skips. It did not promote a candidate by pixel
  change; the user's preference for LaMa was recorded as a method preference,
  not blanket acceptance of masks or framing.
- The user approved the Angelica v7 example, then a 10-image sample tested
  generalization. Its first pass exposed missed reel panels, a false-positive
  rose and incomplete pill cleanup. The revised pattern profiles were
  inspected row by row: all seven reel screenshots were handled, the GMW
  badge was removed, and the rose and clean control stayed untouched.
- The frozen 100-image proof gained reusable patterns for reel frames,
  watermarks, caption blocks, tilted stickers and viewer icons. Each run and
  report remained versioned, with the original v16 baseline unchanged. The
  final review reports explicitly distinguish recommendations, comparison
  images and rejected/intermediate stages.
- Automated checks were useful for report assets, hashes, masks, lineage and
  regressions. The normal validator passed 940 tests across 30 suites; later
  focused checks also passed. Those results supported the visual review but
  did not replace it.

## What was bad

The first 100-image delivery repeated a mistake: I48 was marked complete
before the actual reconstructed composition fed the downstream stages. The
user found that the first crop still retained irrelevant material and the
recommendation still carried borders/UI. Earlier inspection had relied too
much on previews and lineage evidence instead of following each image through
the selected final. I48 was reopened; stage-by-stage inspection and enlarged
native regions then exposed caption gaps and icon strokes that padded previews
hid. This cost another correction cycle and required the user to point out a
failure the assistant should have caught.

The fixed sample also demonstrated that one accepted Angelica repair did not
generalize to other reel layouts or emblems. Even after the later 100-image
proof, residual artifacts, controls, face usability and full-library quality
were not universally accepted. Do not describe the sample as a clean
all-image result.

## What could be improved

- Keep the review gate at the actual recommendation: inspect every row's
  selected final and relevant enlarged regions, not just summary panels or
  passing receipts. Preserve every stage's input/consumer labels. The existing
  bounded sample and acceptance work is tracked in I30-I37 and I47-I53 of the
  [intake plan](../plans/2026-10-23-image-intake-preparation.md).
- Make automated audits do the exhaustive bookkeeping first—coverage, missing
  assets, changed-region boundaries, controls, and per-row failure reasons—
  then present the user only genuine visual ambiguities and policy decisions.
  Keep the quality limits conservative; a smaller Review queue or cleaner
  overlay is not a reason to lower identity thresholds.
- Preserve the pause after a reviewable report. The later pattern iterations
  show why a fixed, labelled sample and an explicit stop condition are better
  than repeatedly expanding scope.

These are refinements to existing intake acceptance work, not new permission
to run a full rebase or change the production recipe.

## Recurring themes

As in the earlier recaps, deterministic artifacts and reproducible checks
helped, but humans remained necessary for synthetic-image quality and
identity decisions. A passing test is evidence that the specified behavior
works; it is not evidence that the chosen crop or reconstruction looks right.
The feedback loop was productive when a specific report was reviewed before
the next broad iteration.

## Complexity and debt

The experimental flow now has multiple layout profiles and many immutable
proof/review runs. Keep recipe, mask and report versions explicit; avoid
growing one-off branches or treating old reports as evidence for newer pixels.
Remaining debt includes confirmed-reference coverage, subject-free margin
handling, face/Junk/replacement gates, enhancement calibration and the
full-run/rebase audit (I41, I44-I46, I49, I54, I60 and C8/C9).
